MTE-422 Converter

A 5 V TTL/UART to RS-422 differential converter built around two MAX485-family transceivers, with a 5-position MTE header on the logic side and a 6-position MTE header on the field side.

Notes

  1. Levels. J1 is 5 V TTL/CMOS UART, idle high. It is not RS-232 — do not wire it to a PC serial port without an RS-232 transceiver in between.
  2. Nets by name. +5V and GND are joined by name across the sheet. Wires that cross without a junction dot are not connected.
  3. Supply. 5.0 V ±5% at J1-1, about 12 mA idle and 60 mA with the driver loaded into a terminated pair. Reversing the supply forward-biases D1, which trips F1 rather than the transceivers.
  4. Driver enable. R3 holds TXEN high, so leaving J1-4 open gives a permanently enabled driver — the normal case for point-to-point RS-422. Drive it low to release the pair when several boards share one transmit bus, and turn the driver off only after the last stop bit has cleared.
  5. Fail-safe bias. With RT2 fitted, RB1/RB2 hold about 283 mV across the receive pair when the cable is open or every driver is idle — above the ±200 mV receiver threshold, so RXD idles high instead of chattering. If the receive pair ends up terminated at both ends (60 Ω effective), change RB1/RB2 to 560 Ω to keep roughly 254 mV.
  6. Termination. Terminate each pair once, at the listening end, with 120 Ω matched to the cable. Fitting a second termination halves the differential swing and costs you noise margin.
  7. Ground and shield. RS-422 needs a third conductor: SG keeps both ends within the receivers’ ±7 V common-mode range. R9 lets it do that without becoming a ground loop. Bond the shield at one end of the run only — bonding both ends turns the shield into a current path.
  8. Cable. Two shielded twisted pairs, 100–120 Ω, 24–22 AWG (Belden 9729 or 8723 class). Keep TX+/TX− on one pair and RX+/RX− on the other; splitting a differential signal across two pairs destroys the common-mode rejection this circuit is built on.
  9. Distance. Roughly 1200 m at 100 kbps, 120 m at 1 Mbps. RS-422 allows one driver and up to ten receivers per pair.
  10. 3.3 V hosts. Substitute MAX3485 for U1 and U2 and supply 3.3 V — same pinout, same layout. Do not feed a 5 V RO output straight into a 3.3 V input.
  11. Isolation. This design is non-isolated and assumes both ends share a ground. For separately powered equipment, long outdoor runs, or anything near line-voltage switching, use an isolated transceiver (ADM2582E, ISO3082) with an isolated DC-DC instead.
  12. Layout. Route each pair as a parallel, equal-length couple over an unbroken ground pour; keep D3–D6 and their ground vias within a few millimetres of J2, and the decoupling caps within 5 mm of the VCC pins.
Before you build it: this is a paper design — it has not been fabricated or simulated. Check U1/U2 pin numbers against the datasheet for the exact package you buy, and confirm the MTE header and mating-housing part numbers against the current TE catalogue, since the series carries different numbers for vertical, right-angle, and plating variants.
